Figure 1. Study design and prespecified parallel analytical layers.
The blood discovery analysis included 30 participants with established steroid-induced osteonecrosis of the femoral head (SONFH) and 10 glucocorticoid-exposed participants without SONFH from GSE123568. Deposited robust multi-array average (RMA) expression and archived GPL15207 annotation were combined for probe-to-gene median aggregation, yielding 18,833 genes across 40 samples. The literature-guided panel contained 85 frozen gene sets across six predefined pathophysiological domains and 28 functional groups; 21 groups contained at least two sets. Layer 1 tested individual gene-set associations, Layer 2 tested differences in disease-associated effects among related sets, and Layer 3 tested 11 predefined opposing-process contrasts. Layers addressed distinct questions rather than a sequential significance-filtering procedure, with separate Benjamini-Hochberg (BH) correction families of 85, 21 and 11 tests. Constituent scores in Layers 2 and 3 were standardized across all 40 participants. Solid, dashed and dotted borders distinguish the three layers independently of color. Blood-based follow-up analyses characterized candidate signals; the exploratory tissue analysis used five SONFH samples and four fracture controls from GSE345158. The aim was to prioritize transcriptional candidates for longitudinal evaluation. Scores represent relative transcriptional patterns rather than direct measurements of physiological function, enzyme activity or metabolic flux. GSVA, gene set variation analysis; HC3, heteroskedasticity-consistent covariance estimator; CR2, cluster-robust covariance estimator with small-sample correction; GC, glucocorticoid.

Figure 2. Individual functional gene-set associations with established SONFH.
(A) Sex-adjusted disease coefficients and HC3-based nominal 95% confidence intervals for all eight gene sets meeting BH false discovery rate (FDR) <0.05 among the 85 prespecified tests. Coefficients use original GSVA score units and compare SONFH with glucocorticoid-exposed non-SONFH participants; the dashed line denotes zero. Short labels correspond to the full identifiers in Supplementary Tables S1 and S2. (B) Scores for the same gene sets in the same row order. Each column denotes one participant. The 10 exposed controls precede the 30 SONFH cases, with archived sample order retained within each group; no clustering was applied. Rows were standardized by their mean and sample standard deviation across all 40 participants. Blue indicates below-cohort-mean scores and orange indicates above-cohort-mean scores for the respective gene set. The heatmap displays unadjusted individual scores descriptively and is not an independent validation of the selected associations. GC, glucocorticoid; CI, confidence interval.

Figure 3. Comparisons among related functional gene sets and opposing-process contrasts.
(A) BH FDR values for all 21 prespecified functional groups containing at least two gene sets. Group tests evaluated equality of sex-adjusted disease coefficients for constituent scores standardized across all 40 participants, using participant-clustered CR2 covariance and Satterthwaite or Hotelling T-squared (HTZ) tests as appropriate. The dotted orange line denotes FDR=0.05; no group met this threshold. Rows follow domain order and alphabetical group order within domains, with faint lines separating domains. (B) Sex-adjusted disease coefficients and HC3-based nominal 95% confidence intervals for all 11 prespecified opposing-process contrasts, defined as z(A)-z(B). Constituent scores were standardized across all 40 participants; contrasts were not restandardized. Coefficients compare SONFH with exposed non-SONFH participants. The dashed line denotes zero. The orange diamond identifies the only contrast meeting FDR<0.05 across 11 tests; gray circles denote the remaining contrasts. Confidence intervals are not multiplicity-adjusted. Failure to meet an FDR threshold does not establish equal effects. The two panels use separate correction families. Full definitions and results are provided in Supplementary Tables S3 and S4.

Figure 4. Component-level characterization of the hydrogen peroxide contrast.
(A) Individual biosynthesis-associated scores, catabolism-associated scores and biosynthesis-minus-catabolism contrasts in 10 glucocorticoid-exposed non-SONFH participants and 30 SONFH participants. Each component was standardized using its mean and sample standard deviation across all 40 participants; the contrast was not restandardized. Boxes show the median and Tukey hinges as implemented by R boxplot.stats; whiskers extend to the most extreme observations within 1.5 interquartile ranges of the hinges. All participant values are overlaid with deterministic horizontal offsets. Distributions are unadjusted and descriptive, with common vertical limits. (B) Disease coefficients and HC3-based nominal 95% confidence intervals from separate sex-adjusted models for both components and their contrast. Component P values are exploratory and unadjusted for multiplicity. Contrast FDR was calculated across all 11 prespecified opposing-process contrasts rather than across the three displayed rows. The contrast coefficient equals the biosynthesis coefficient minus the catabolism coefficient; standard errors and confidence intervals cannot be subtracted in this manner. The contrast association was predominantly attributable to lower catabolism-associated expression. These data do not establish increased peroxide generation, impaired clearance, physiological redox imbalance or superior diagnostic performance.

Figure 5. Sensitivity and interpretation of the hydrogen peroxide-related transcriptional features.
(A) Sex-adjusted coefficients and HC3-based nominal 95% confidence intervals for the biosynthesis-minus-catabolism contrast using GSVA or single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA). Constituent scores were standardized separately under each scoring method across all 40 participants; contrasts were not restandardized. FDR was calculated separately across all 11 prespecified contrasts under each method. These estimates do not constitute a formal test of differences between scoring methods. (B) Catabolism-associated scores recalculated after removing SNCA, PRDX2, both genes, MPO and EPX, or all four genes. Original GSVA score units and exploratory nominal P values are shown. All four refers to SNCA, PRDX2, MPO and EPX; these scenarios are not a new primary FDR family. (C) Four-gene erythroid expression proxy, defined as the mean of expression z scores for ALAS2, AHSP, GYPA and SLC4A1, versus the catabolism-associated GSVA score. The pooled Spearman coefficient is descriptive; colors identify exposed non-SONFH participants and SONFH cases. (D) Sex-adjusted catabolism coefficients before and after additional adjustment for the four-gene proxy or an exploratory five-gene proxy including HBB. The four genes are outside the frozen catabolism set; HBB overlaps that set and is included only in a separate sensitivity version. P values and confidence intervals in B and D are nominal and unadjusted for multiplicity. Different panels use different outcome units, so their coefficient magnitudes should not be compared directly. Proxy-related attenuation does not establish causal confounding by cellular composition; the proxy was not validated against measured blood-cell counts. Fixed-score leave-one-out analyses are provided separately.

Figure 6. Exploratory assessment of the hydrogen peroxide contrast in femoral head tissue.
Individual biosynthesis-minus-catabolism contrasts are shown for five SONFH samples and four fracture controls from GSE345158. (A) Original frozen gene sets scored using the symbol-mapped, SOD2-corrected expression matrix. (B) Both sets rescored after removing their shared DUOX1 and DUOX2 genes. GSVA component scores were standardized separately within each version across all nine tissue samples; differences were not restandardized. Points denote individual samples and horizontal bars denote group means, not confidence intervals. Mean differences compare SONFH with fracture controls. Two-sided exact P values enumerate all 126 allocations of five case labels among nine samples and count absolute mean differences at least as large as observed, including the observed allocation. These nominal exploratory tests were outside the primary blood FDR families. Removing DUOX1 and DUOX2 did not reduce the observed mean difference, although the exact P value crossed the nominal 0.05 threshold. The small tissue sample and different control population support exploratory assessment of direction rather than matched independent validation of a blood biomarker. Tissue scores do not directly measure enzyme activity, peroxide clearance or physiological redox balance.
